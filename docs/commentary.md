# From the start to the wire

This is a fictional six-runner, four-lap race. With **Sound on**, the caller knows which silks are in front, which runners moved, and who actually crossed. He is not reading a script for a predetermined winner. Under **Race monitor → Calls** the last three calls remain available as captions.

## Before the gates · the paddock microphone

The sixty-second open book has six calls in cloth order and a short wrap. Each call joins a recorded roster name, the horse’s **printed running style**, and a line about its highest gate rating. Equal ratings use the printed stat order. No result, stake or forecast is read to make this introduction. An older Strong finisher gets the less specific late-run cue, not a promise under today’s rules. The late closer on a new card gets the two-kick call for laps three and four; an already-issued card keeps its original late-closer cue. The console marks the current runner; **Station menu → Paddock report** keeps all six descriptions available in print.

The first slot opens at 0.4 seconds; slots are 8.65 seconds apart. Turning on sound joins the current slot if the whole line still fits. Manual Start, automatic lock, mute, a paused folio and a hidden tab stop the scheduled sources; a new card never inherits a queued introduction. `paddock-radio.ogg` contains fifteen aligned original British cues and reuses the existing fifty local name recordings, not browser or network voices.

## Delivery and ticket prices

- Each runner's spoken name and every call are local audio in the same voice. Captions do not require a sound device. The mixes at opening / lap two / lap three / bell / home use voice gains **0.43 / 0.51 / 0.61 / 0.73 / 0.88** and playback rates **0.97 / 1.01 / 1.055 / 1.10 / 1.17**. Shorter gaps between calls increase urgency; hoof microphones dip under speech. Measured across the original 20 recorded phrase segments in each delivery group, the staged voice rises from −24.7 to −23.2, −21.0, −20.1 and −17.9 dBFS before the master volume. The final step is tied to the surveyed start of the actual home straight, not to a timer.
- The caller observes half-second snapshots; post, lap and turn-in cues are dated to the crossing they report. A changing leader, an active sprint, a shrinking gap, and a rally from the back have different eligibility rules. No line repeats in one heat. Mute, pause, a hidden tab and the next heat cannot build up a backlog.
- A fresh gate book uses **2,048** independent, reproducible trials of the same race law. Each sample supplies the *ordered* first two. During the race, samples with similar relative distances and the observed tempo receive more weight, updated once per race second and immediately on a finish. Only confirmed placings can decide a ticket. A very old, unrated saved heat uses its field weights and observed gaps instead of invented ratings.
- **Win** = first. **Show** = exactly second in this cabinet. **Quinella** = the two selections finish first and second in either order. The chance belongs to the whole ticket. A Quinella is **not** the product of two win percentages. Chance is neither a guarantee nor a revised price. **20 @ 12×** means 240 returned and **+220 net if hit**; a losing ticket costs 20. The posted odds, draw, balance and receipt are untouched.
