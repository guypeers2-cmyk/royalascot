# Royal Ascot · Harness Trot

A playable mechanical medal derby at Station 07. It is a local, single-player simulation, not a casino stream, online multiplayer or cash game. Station 07 is yours; the other stools are empty.

## Play the sulky edition

**Edition 17 — Rail panorama.** Six full-width racing lines with a continuous half-lane shoulder on each side. [Dimensions, motion, optics and research](docs/panorama.md).

Open `royal-ascot.html`. **Win** is selected on every entry to the betting console, on reload and on each newly opened field. Choose your medal stake above the field. Click a horse once to prepare a slip; double-click or double-tap it to place that stake **once**. For **Quinella**, select the first horse, then a different horse. Use **Place bet**, or double-click the second horse. The book on the right is the list of tickets actually placed, not a preview. Its × key refunds a ticket while betting is open; on small glass use **My bets → Cancel**. To browse every pair without committing a stake, choose **Station menu → All 15 Quinella prices**.

Rail uses a fixed lens for each screen layout. It pans through the parade, loading and race without zooming when a horse is pinned or the pack stretches. It fits all six teams in the unobstructed glass. Action cam is a separate automatic lens for a duel, a sprint or the leading pair. Console still offers a static overview. Your selected camera **stays live through the last turn and all six crossings**. The camera menu, seat gaze, rail pin and stop-follow key still work there. No finish camera takes the controls. The separate perpendicular sensor records the official first-crossing photo; open **Photo & result** to see it.

The low, two-sided infield board shows just six silk numbers: programme order at the gate, actual running order on the track, official order at the finish. The separate station glasses and Race monitor keep their names and details. The scoreboard stands halfway between the two inner rails and the two turns. Four low flower beds wrap around its feet. All share one survey, from the printed camera to the orbit. Runner files and race cards are printed on opaque stock; the separate Live book always has a 25%-opaque background and fully opaque lettering.

Six unmounted horses pull original two-wheeled racing sulkies. Each driver sits behind the horse with two reins, legs extended forward and both soles supported on inboard stirrups and their own racing colours. During the race the horses **trot**: left fore and right hind load together, then right fore and left hind, with a short suspension between supports. The fore knee stays nearly straight under load; the high stifle leads forward on recovery, above and in front of the rear-pointing hock. Their opposite bends close together as the hind foot comes underneath. Orbit, 3D Action, the 28 photographic bearings and both detailed flanks use the same distance-driven motion. The four-beat walk to the stalls, quiet stand and original finishing times are separate. The v13 fetlock now sinks at midstance, recoils and rolls the heel off the contacting toe. Both diagonal partners now share lift, sweep, pitch and heel rise; the fore knee and hind hock retain their own linkage. New cards use v17 stock; v1–v16 prints retain their own exposures. [Draf: bewegingsstudie en controle (NL)](docs/diagonal-trot.md).

## Race radio and live tickets

Switch **Sound on** (or press **M**) for the British race caller. During betting he works through all six printed starters, naming each one, its style and a strength from its actual gate ratings. The console’s printed stat figures are one CSS pixel larger at every text size. Horse names on the console are one CSS pixel smaller without shrinking the pick buttons or ratings. The **Paddock report** in the station menu is readable even with sound off. Closing betting interrupts the review immediately. Once the gates open, 136 original English calls, including 36 dry British asides, are matched to observable race events. The mic builds through four laps and peaks as the leader exits the last bend. A tight finish is not a win until a horse crosses. All 50 runner names are in the local voice bank; no internet or installed voice is required in a browser with Opus support. Read [the research notes and all 136 calls](docs/commentary.md).

**My bets** is left of **Race monitor**, whether over the track or docked. A 20-medal ticket at 14.4× shows **+268 net if hit**; the odds stay locked even as its hit chance changes. One model estimates first, exactly second and both orders of a top-two pair. Percentages are estimates until the relevant places are settled, not promises or new prices.

## Course and race model

**Six lanes, exactly 1.2 racing bikes each.** Outer cap to outer cap: 7.858 cm on the cabinet. Lane pitch: 9.429 cm, giving 0.786 cm either side of a centred sulky; six lanes occupy 56.575 cm. Outside this active strip, each shoulder measures another 4.715 cm to its rail, for 66.005 cm in all. The outer casing and 3.40 m straights stay fixed. The inside rail, start bays, hoof scuffs, mower chart, side photographs, map, Orbit and new judges’ exposure follow that same survey. Steering uses a rigid horse-to-axle chord through both tangencies, not a tangent which swings the axle across the neighbouring lane. [Construction, research and measurements](docs/racing-bike.md).

**Horses and track.** The pulling horse is 20% larger than v14 in all live views: scale 0.80 → 0.96 (0.21024 model metres per gait unit). The cart is additional length, not a replacement for that increase. The wide Rail lens is independent of the figure size and protects the whole running field. The contact print includes the wheels. Each lane has an asymmetric 32.9 cm start bay: 4.5 cm behind the rearmost point of the helmet or tyre, 3.7 cm in front of the muzzle and 3.8 cm above the team under the header. The rear paddles wait for the final wheel; the suspended partitions leave the wheel path open when turning out. On the rail and oblique seat film the posts use the figures’ local scale rather than the foreshortened scale of track travel. The start line, team size and clock are unchanged. The new cloths and catalogue pictures show the actual team. A four-beat walk brings them up the home straight; feet settle under the body before the latch and the push-off eases into the race stride. In orbit, the loaded feet set the ground plane and the cast leans into a bend according to its curvature and actual speed. Raised fibre tufts follow the two clipped verges in one track mesh. Timing ticks terminate at the same surveyed line in both the photographic view and the maquette.

**Fixed skill, fresh condition.** Every one of the 50 named horses keeps **165 points split between sprint speed and cruising pace**, independent of silk number, heat or station. Pace setters favour cruise; late closers favour sprint; all-rounders sit near 82/83. On the opening card Susy Spitfire carries 100 pace and Waregem Flyer 100 sprint. A small per-name offset distinguishes horses of the same style. **Fitness and starting stamina are separate 80–100 draws (inclusive)**. Both can be 100 on the same start; a high FIT no longer forces a low STA. Both differ from that horse’s last start, including warm-ups. All four gate figures feed the forecast and the 64 sectors. Live stamina adjusts usable start, pace and sprint figures. On new cards live FIT has its own elapsed-time account; neither balance rewrites the printed skills. Prices use a separate play-medal book. New Win, Show and Quinella multipliers are **20% above** the preceding printed book; the forecast is separate. On the new R043 card #1/#3 pays **12×**, #1/#2 **14.4×**, #4/#5 **68.4×**. Win #1 is **6×**, #5 **18×**. Gross includes stake. The posted board, accepted tickets and receipts are never repriced. Editing a ticket, changing camera or reloading never redraws a field. Saved pre-Turf cards, accepted prices and receipts are honoured. Only the next newly built card takes the new draw.

**At the rail.** One brass-edged dark-green panel appears at the **bottom right** once the horses are standing in the boxes (−5 s). During racing, the **Race monitor column is 5% wider** than in Parterre. My bets keeps its previous width, both in overlay and dock. The boxed-stall layout is unchanged. Only the green substrate is **75% transparent**; names, figures and controls remain fully opaque, on the track or docked. No mesh collision scan is needed to choose the opacity. On the right, **Race monitor** shows all six stalls with running style and the actual starting reserves. At release it switches to observed running order and lap. Style stays printed separately from **STA** (actual stamina remaining) and drive. **Saving** has a translucent green badge, **Sprint** bright yellow with dark lettering and **Spent** red. Spent means its sprint windows are over, not that its reserve is empty. Hover a trait to read what it does. **My bets** is on its left: market, coloured selection(s), stake, locked decimal odds, net profit if the ticket hits, and an estimated chance for the whole ticket. It starts at the gate and updates with the observed race. Two adjacent silks inside one outline are a single Quinella. Tap either silk in the race to follow that horse. **Stop follow** in the My bets header restores the field without changing a ticket. It is still available when the slips are folded. The monitor’s Tickets page repeats those terms and chances. **Dock** moves the same panel into a separate right-hand strip, below the film on narrow screens. The camera resizes and frames the stalls; all six tickets stay open. **Overlay** puts it back. The station remembers the position when browser storage is available. The sections still fold independently. The panel is absent while betting, during the walk-in and on the result screen. The separate **My bets** book prints one line per ticket: market, cloth number(s), stake, locked price, potential return and refund key. It sorts Win → Show → Quinella without reordering the ledger. The stake keys above Field offer **5 · 10 · 20 · 50 · 100 · 200**. One click selects the amount without spending it. Double-click (or double-tap) that same amount to stake it once on the selected runner or Quinella; **Place bet** remains available. Both routes use the same cashier and posted odds. Stamina uses three bands: **≤20% red, ≥80% green, everything between orange**, with the percentage printed beside the colour. Printed traits retain their separate rating scale; missing readings are dashes.

**Sprint plans.** Each of the four laps has 16 sectors. A front runner sprints through the first half of lap 1. A pace setter makes one 6–9-sector move at a random point in the race. An all-rounder makes two quarter-lap moves, starting halfway through lap 2 and halfway through lap 3. On a new card (law 12), a strong finisher sprints through **the first quarter of lap 1**, saves until the bell, and sprints **the entire fourth lap**: [0,4), [48,64). Law 8–10 strong finishers keep [48,64) alone. An already-issued law-7 card retains 52–64; earlier cards retain their own marker. A steady pacer makes a short move at a random point in each of the four laps. A late closer makes two distinct half-lap sprints: halfway through lap 3 to its end **[40,48)**, saves through the first half of lap 4, then sprints to the finish **[56,64)**. A booked law-8 card still uses its single [48,60) move. New cards apply **75% of the previous sprint speed** at the same usable ratings. At usable SPR 80 the nominal multiplier is 1.5× instead of 2×. This is sector speed, not just faster leg animation. Stamina while sprinting spends **3 percentage points per elapsed race second**; all other running spends **0.025 points per second**, even after the last sprint. There is no banking or recharge. As an example, 10 seconds from 90% leave 60% sprinting or 89.75% saving. A runner can hit zero between sector markers. Fitness is separate: new cards lose **1 FIT point/s in a sprint**, **0.025 FIT points/s otherwise**. For example, 90 FIT becomes 80 after ten sprint seconds, versus 89.75 when saving. Read live FIT in the runner file and gate + four lap readings in the archive. Both are clamped at zero. Pausing freezes the clock, and each horse stops spending at its own finish. Remaining reserve limits usable fitness, stamina, sprint speed and pace on every sector. The live runner file shows these stamina-adjusted values; printed and archived entries retain the actual gate ratings. These intervals change speed and reserve, feed the forecast, and are saved with crossings. An already posted card keeps its original stamina rules, forecast and prices; saved runs and receipts are not recalculated.

**Calibration.** The six loopwijzes keep their different sprint windows. Law 12 calibrates the saving strides of each fixed build at 90 FIT / 90 STA over sixteen phases of each rhythm. A rhythm keeps 45% of its natural time advantage; the algorithm does not read today's field, bets or placings. In 12,000 matched opening-profile races all six styles won **16.4–17.3%**. The front runner led lap one 97.7% of the time; strong finishers gained 2.51 places on lap four, and late closers 1.41. Over another 12,000 matched races with all 50 horses and fresh gate condition: front 16.9%, strong 16.4%, pace setter 16.0%, late 15.7%, all-rounder 17.6%, steady 17.4%. These are controlled comparisons, not promised odds.

**The Private Drawing Room.** A separate, enclosed room with black panels and doors, brushed gold mouldings, five groups of settees and low tables, and warm shaded lamps. There is no bar or public gaming floor around the machine. Choose **Cabinet orbit** to inspect it. Three outward-facing terminals on each straight and two on each bend give ten working glasses and ten empty stools; station 07 is the centre of the near bank. The six numbers on the low infield strip are independent of the named top-three display on the public screens. The cloth numbers ride on their actual flanks, including the reverse flank on the far straight. The printed digit alone is 30% smaller on the live cloth and on new v10 negatives; it does not shrink the number plates or map. The archived v9 negative keeps the original ink size and uses the furnished lounge; v8 retains the previous salon, and v7 still develops on its original [casino-gallery stock](CREDITS.md#casino-gallery--photographic-references).

**Two sound cues.** In *How to play → Sound*, “Before the start” fires at the betting lock, ten seconds before release; “Opening gates” fires once at release and cuts off the first clip. They accept separate local recordings (up to 60 s / 16 MB and 10 s / 4 MB respectively, minimum 0.05 s), for this session only. The station starts muted. The defaults are the two supplied recordings: a 7.595 s countdown fanfare and a 2.043 s metal-gate sound. The player uses the original MP3 and, on AAC-capable browsers, the original M4A. A local 16-bit FLAC of that same fanfare covers browsers without AAC. No trimming, tempo change or added silence. The synthesized signals are codec fallbacks only. Both recordings are embedded in the standalone HTML; no Drive connection or plugin is needed.

**Race sound.** Cloths 1–6 each use a different real race-meeting recording, not an oscillator. Six local, independently panned voices track speed and position; follow a horse to bring its sound forward. Each voice stops at that horse’s finish. Mute, paused folios and backgrounding also silence the mix. See [recordists, original pages and CC0 licenses](CREDITS.md#recorded-hoofbeats--six-trackside-voices).

The [previous Turf notes](docs/turf.md) describe the earlier grass and race-form edition. The separate [@architect workbook](architect/index.html) is a reusable, offline chatbot planning tool with 24 virtual roles, including the four-role WebGL / 3D render studio, a flowchart and an executable local dispatcher; see [its own README](architect/README.md). ## Race model and checkpoints

Each new gate entry has two independent condition integers in [80,100] and two permanent skills in [65,100]. The named horse and style determine the 165-point sprint/pace split; distinct hashes of the horse, heat and entry seed supply FIT and STA. Each draw excludes only that trait’s own last-start reading. No common condition pool; issuing a card spends no extra market or outcome draw. Already issued fields (including an empty cashier), existing curves and archival snapshots are not rewritten. At the same remaining stamina FIT does not add to the usable PAC or SPR number. Instead it sustains the cruising speed more in later sectors (the effect depends on the race rhythm). Sprint travel is built on that same base speed: the multiplier is **0.75 × (2 + 0.008 × (usable SPR − 80))**. Thus better fitness can speed up the actual sprint without increasing its multiplier. Fixed skills are balanced at reference FIT/STA 90/90, not rebalanced for today’s condition. Starting reserve, the break, cruising pace and a stamina-limited kick affect each crossing; they do not change the two drain rates. No rubber-banding or ticket-dependent draw. A reproducible 2,048-trial forecast has a separate sampler; odds are based on the original pricing weights. A cold station simulates six-runner heats until every one of the 50 names has five finishes. That takes 42 balanced heats (252 actual starts) before Race 043. Older stations recover known receipts first, then run only missing pre-season qualifiers on their own number series. Their positions go behind existing form, without renumbering or redrawing the current race. A complete book consumes no draws on reload. Up to 40 receipts and the independent per-name stud book persist locally. Old paid races retain their curves and locked prices. Settlement occurs once.

`tools/prepare_side_plate.py` works from the original masters through the long-course reframe into `broad_turf.py` / `broad_rails.py` (NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV, Pillow and DejaVu Sans). It builds the world-space cut chart, the new side plate, its two occlusion films and the empty background used on the console. `tools/prepare_seat_pov.py` reprojects the five stationary faces. `tools/prepare_judges_plate.py` registers the v6 upper gallery to the existing broad wire plate **without rewriting** any v1–v5 film stock. For the current private room, `python tools/salon_room.py --stock /path/to/5f19578/assets --private` uses the shipped panorama to reproject the cabinet plate, five seat faces and the distinct v13 wire strip. Rebuild the glossy-room lighting with `node tools/bake_room_light.cjs /tmp/light.png` and lossless WebP. `node tools/bake_line.cjs` renders the empty final-straight plate through the same lens as the live horses. Neither tool rewrites the v1–v8 wire stock when developing the lounge. `node tools/bake_carriers.cjs --stalls` generates the six neutral standing atlases. With no switch it develops the live motion sheets with `tools/pack_gait.py`; set `ASCOT_STUDIO` to keep the ungraded passes. `--walk` bakes the six eight-exposure four-beat walks and packs one common matte plus five pigment grades. These heavy art tools are **not** needed to play, host or build the offline HTML. Do not apply the silk grades a second time to packed sheets.

From a clean Git checkout of local `main`, `python tools/export_release.py /path/to/output` regenerates the standalone game, static hosting ZIP, full root-ready source and GitHub ZIP, complete history bundle and separate architect kit. No remote is contacted. The source ZIP and bundle both include the original `art-src/` masters. The space-limited authoring checkout can omit masters and sealed legacy plates. `npm start` and the offline packer read intentionally skipped negatives from the local Git index, not the network. `git sparse-checkout disable` materializes the full repository; all downloads already contain their full intended contents.

**Reviewing Action cadence.** With the development server and Playwright available, `node tools/action_audit.cjs` measures actual camera paints and frame-time percentiles while the race clock runs. `ASCOT_RENDERER=disabled` tests the no-WebGL path; `native` uses the available browser graphics. `ASCOT_QUALITY=low|medium|high|auto` selects the global profile. `ASCOT_ACTION_PICTURE=3d` explicitly exercises the moving model. For a like-for-like comparison, pass the same racing checkpoint via `ASCOT_CHECKPOINT` and select each source tree via `ASCOT_SITE`. Run one audit at a time. These are browser measurements, not a guaranteed frame rate on another device.
